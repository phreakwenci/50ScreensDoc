The Story

At its core, 50 Screens is a national portrait told through local stories. Rather than focusing solely on decline, the film seeks out the tension between crisis and reinvention.

Can independent theaters survive — and if so, what will they become?

Dispatches

From the Road

◆ March 14, 2025

The Rialto, Macon — "She introduces every show."

Margaret Holloway is 82. She stands at the front of the auditorium before every Friday screening and tells the audience what they're about to see and why it matters. Not from notes. From memory. The theater has been in her family since 1946. We filmed three nights here. On the third night, she let us into the projection booth alone.

◆ February 28, 2025

Sheridan, WY — Rebuilding by hand.

The Starlight Drive-In took a direct hit in the '97 storm. Tommy Palomino and his sons rebuilt the screen themselves over nine months — no contractors, no insurance payout. "You don't hire someone to fix your own home," he said. The new screen is crooked if you look close. Nobody cares. The cars still come.

◆ January 12, 2025

Bellows Falls, VT — A barn that shows movies.

First shoot of the year. The Roxie was a dairy barn until 1948 — you can still see the hayloft ceiling from inside the auditorium. The Coopers run it with six volunteers, all over sixty. Ruth Cooper told us: "The cows are gone. The movies stayed. That's the whole story." It isn't, of course. That's never the whole story.
