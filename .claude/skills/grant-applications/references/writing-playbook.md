# 50 Screens — Application Writing Playbook

The house style, distilled from real reviewer feedback on the Film Independent Doc Lab
application. **Run every draft through this before presenting it**, and use it to
critique existing drafts. Each rule has the failure it prevents.

## The core rules

1. **Kill boilerplate. Be specific to this film and this person.**
   Phrases that describe *any* producer on *any* project are dead weight to a reviewer who
   reads hundreds of applications. Banned unless made concrete: "leave no stone unturned,"
   "work closely with the director to communicate their vision," "we are a safe set."
   Replace with a specific fact, relationship, or detail only 50 Screens could claim.

2. **Lead differentiation with embedded access, not nostalgia.**
   "We grew up watching movies in neighborhood theaters" is true of nearly every
   filmmaker. The differentiators here are concrete: Richard's years as a **projectionist
   at AFI Silver Theatre**; Jan-David's **Pasadena, Maryland** roots and the Loews Theater
   that carried him through chemo; specific named theater relationships (Pike Cinema,
   Gardena, Plaza, Moore). Name them.

3. **Engage the systemic forces with real specifics, not vibes.**
   A topic summary must do more than describe what the film covers. Show a point of view
   on *why now*: post-COVID consolidation of exhibition, streaming economics, the film-to-
   digital transition, and what the data actually shows about independent-theater closures.
   Name the stakes concretely — what a specific community loses when its theater closes and
   what fills the void — not "communities are affected."

4. **Answer the actual question the section asks.**
   - "Ethics/accountability" = the **filmmaker–subject relationship** (ongoing consent,
     transparency about use, ability to flag concerns before lock) — NOT door signage,
     releases, or logistics.
   - "Community care/safety" = the **emotional and reputational wellbeing** of vulnerable
     subjects — NOT (only) physical set safety.
   - When a prompt asks a precise question (e.g., "Are you a Fellow?"), answer it plainly.
     Don't guess or bluff ("I presume I will be one if I sign up?" reads as not knowing
     what the program is — the answer was simply "No").

5. **Funding honesty ladder: Secured / Applied / Planned.**
   - **Secured** = money confirmed in hand/committed.
   - **Applied** = actually submitted, awaiting decision.
   - **Planned** = intend to submit; give the target window.
   Never write "in process" for an unsubmitted grant, and never list an amount a program
   would not award at that stage (a reviewer who knows the landscape will catch it and
   discount the whole section). When unsure, ask; do not inflate.

6. **Always surface the Nouveau Roadshow distribution hook.**
   The company building its theatrical-distribution network *during* production — every
   theater filmed becoming a release partner, every theater's audience a built-in
   constituency — is the single most differentiating element. If a distribution, audience,
   or "why this project" section omits it, the draft is incomplete.

7. **Right-fit platforms only.**
   For a festival-track arthouse documentary, name PBS/Independent Lens (esp. with ITVS),
   Kanopy/Swank (educational), and MUBI/Criterion Channel (premium arthouse). **Do not**
   list ad-supported services (Tubi, Pluto) — the mismatch signals the team hasn't thought
   about positioning.

8. **Match the funder's priorities and rules.**
   Read eligibility before drafting. Examples that have mattered here: FI's applicant
   **cannot be the director**; **Roy W. Dean** caps budgets under $500K (use the "minus
   $500K" budget); **NEH does not require a nonprofit** (leans cultural-preservation);
   ITVS Open Call needs **WIP footage**. Tailor emphasis to what the funder actually funds.

9. **Attach what's required, in the right format.**
   For grant readers, submit the **Budget Topsheet as a clean PDF** (show the $614,451
   total at the top), not the full line-item detail. Verify every link/password works and
   every required field is filled — a broken work-sample link can sink an application.

## Quick self-check before presenting a draft
- [ ] Would this sentence be false for any other film? If not, cut or specify it.
- [ ] Did I name concrete access (AFI Silver / specific theaters / specific people)?
- [ ] Does each section answer the *actual* question asked?
- [ ] Is every dollar labeled Secured / Applied / Planned, and is every figure realistic?
- [ ] Is Nouveau Roadshow present in the distribution/audience framing?
- [ ] Platforms right-fit for a festival arthouse doc?
- [ ] Within the word/character limit, and consistent with `project-facts.md`?
- [ ] Attachments correct (Topsheet PDF, working sample link), required fields complete?

## Tone
Confident but not blunt-polemical. Let the film's subjects carry the argument about
corporate consolidation rather than editorializing in the applicant's voice. Intimate,
specific, grounded — the same values as the film itself.
